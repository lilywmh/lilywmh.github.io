Phase 1 · Study Design

The same conversations, viewed through three channels

Strangers completed two counterbalanced co-viewing sessions. Written reflections separated initial interpretation from the conversation that followed; synchronized transcript, audio, and video then allowed each channel to be evaluated on the same interaction.

Phase 1 · Completed

Fine-grained floor exchange is the clearest signal so far

The earlier multimodal project evaluated transcript, acoustic, and visual features through a measurement-first sequence: detect the intended interaction pattern, establish stable measurement, then test association with conversation evaluation. The most replicable construct was not content similarity or a vocal alignment index. It was the granularity of floor exchange.

Conversations with more speaker switches, greater switch density, and shorter turns received more positive evaluations. These are not three separate discoveries: they are strongly correlated indicators of one floor-exchange construct. The same construct-level direction appeared in CANDOR, making the pattern more credible while keeping the small CVS sample and outcome uncertainty in view.

Phase 2 · In Progress

A busy conversation is not necessarily a responsive one

Frequent switching can reflect mutual engagement, but it can also come from interruption, one-sided questioning, or one speaker giving only brief replies. Pair-level counts cannot identify whose behavior shaped whose experience.

The next analysis assigns every transition to the person who takes the floor, then retains each partner's evaluation instead of averaging the two reports.

Responsiveness Measures

From turn counts to directed sequences

Each conversation becomes a speaker-directed sequence. Every floor transition is assigned to the person who responds, allowing self- and partner-responsiveness to be separated.

Response latency

How quickly a partner takes the floor after the previous speaker finishes.

Transition quality

Whether the exchange is smooth, silent, or overlapping rather than treating all switches as equivalent.

Turn-duration balance

Whether both partners have comparable opportunities to hold and release the floor.

Reciprocity

Whether rapid exchange is mutual or driven disproportionately by one person.

Analysis Plan

Three gates before a directional claim

01

Interaction-specific structure

Compare timing measures with time-shifted and pseudo-dyad controls where appropriate. A convenient metric must first capture something specific to the interaction.

02

Stability across questions and sessions

Separate momentary timing noise from reliable individual and dyadic differences before interpreting an outcome association.

03

Actor–partner mixed-effects models

Test whether a participant's evaluation is predicted more strongly by the partner's responsiveness than by their own. Models retain person-level ratings, account for repeated observations within dyads, and test timing beyond transcript content.

Prospective boundary: existing CVS data refine the measurement pipeline; hypotheses will be frozen before examining outcomes in the newly collected sample. CANDOR will support a directional replication only if compatible person-level outcomes are available.
