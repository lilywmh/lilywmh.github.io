Background

Anhedonia is hard to measure at scale

Anhedonia — the diminished capacity to experience pleasure or the motivation to pursue rewards — is a transdiagnostic marker of reward-system dysfunction and a strong predictor of treatment resistance in major depressive disorder. Yet measuring it well is expensive: it requires either task-based functional neuroimaging or subjective self-report scales, neither of which scales to routine screening.

Neuroscience localizes anhedonia to dopaminergic circuits projecting to the nucleus accumbens, and depressive speech shows a parallel prosodic flattening — reduced pitch variability, compressed dynamic range, slower rate. If that vocal signature carries the same information as a brain scan, a short recording could become a low-cost screening tool. The catch: prior vocal-biomarker work targets global depression severity, not anhedonia specifically, and mostly relies on proprietary, non-reproducible pipelines.

Question & Hypotheses

Voice vs. brain, head to head

"Can computational analysis of a brief voice recording classify depression-related anhedonia as effectively as task-based fMRI?"

H1 · Above chance

Acoustic prosody classifies anhedonia above chance.

H2 · Non-inferiority

Acoustic classification is non-inferior to fMRI-based classification (ΔAUC lower CI > −0.10).

H3 · Which features

Pitch and energy features rank among the top predictors.

Methods

Two independent classification streams

Because no public dataset pairs naturalistic clinical audio with task-based fMRI and validated anhedonia labels, the two streams draw from separate cohorts — making this a cross-modal benchmark rather than a multimodal fusion. Both were pre-registered before analysis.

🎙️

Stream A — Acoustic Prosody (my lead)

  • Corpus: DAIC-WOZ clinical interviews, n = 142 after QC exclusions (22.5% anhedonic)
  • Pipeline: ClinicalWhisper — Whisper Large-v3, pyannote 3.1 diarization, OpenSMILE eGeMAPS v02
  • Features: 88 acoustic prosody features
  • Label: PHQ-8 items 1+2 ≥ 3 (the "anhedonia composite")
🧠

Stream B — Neural Activation

  • Corpus: UCLA ds000030, n = 234 after exclusions
  • Signal: Nucleus accumbens BOLD during a reward task (BART)
  • Label: Revised Chapman Physical Anhedonia Scale, median split
  • Note: different construct & cohort from Stream A — by design

Shared modeling protocol: three classifiers — logistic regression, random forest, and gradient-boosted trees — evaluated under stratified 5-fold cross-validation with fixed, pre-registered hyperparameters. Above-chance performance was tested by permutation; feature importance by SHAP.

H1 — Supported, with a caveat

Stream A reached AUC 0.63 (random forest, permutation p = .049), confirming above-chance classification at α = .05 uncorrected — though it does not survive Bonferroni correction for three classifiers (α/3 = .017).

H2 — Non-inferiority met, but both were modest

Bootstrap analysis of ΔAUC confirmed non-inferiority relative to Stream B (95% CI lower bound > −0.10). But Stream B itself did not classify above chance (p = .057) — so this reflects comparable, modest performance across modalities, not equivalence to a validated neural biomarker.

H3 — Partially supported

Pitch variability (F0) features ranked among the top-5 predictors by SHAP value in the gradient-boosted trees model. An exploratory combined model (eGeMAPS + recurrence quantification analysis) reached AUC 0.65 (p = .032), though the lift was not significant by DeLong test (z = −0.44, p = .66).

Discussion

What the comparison can and can't claim

A core commitment of the paper is honesty about construct validity. We report the target throughout as an "anhedonia composite" rather than pure anhedonia, and we foreground the design's limits rather than overselling the result.

Neither stream isolates anhedonia

PHQ-8 items 1+2 conflate anhedonia with dysphoria; the BART conflates reward processing with risk-taking and impulsivity.

Different constructs, different cohorts

The two streams use different anhedonia measures on different populations, so the comparison is a benchmark of modalities — not strictly apples-to-apples.

Small samples, arbitrary cutoffs

The RPAS median split dichotomizes a continuous scale, discarding variance and inflating Type II error; results should be read as initial evidence.

Reproducibility as the contribution

ClinicalWhisper is fully open-source and standardized, addressing a field-wide problem of proprietary pipelines that block cross-study comparison.

Takeaway

A five-minute recording carries real signal

These results give initial evidence that vocal prosody — acquired through a standardized, open-source pipeline — carries depression-related information comparable to fMRI-derived ventral striatal activation for binary classification. The honest framing is that both modalities performed modestly and neither measure isolates anhedonia cleanly. The path forward is a stronger operationalization of the construct and a properly powered, confirmatory replication.
